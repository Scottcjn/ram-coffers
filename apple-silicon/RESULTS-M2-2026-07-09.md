# NEON TBL Collapse — First Validation on Apple M2 (2026-07-09)

First run of the AArch64 non-bijunctive collapse engine on real Apple silicon.
Hardware: Mac Mini M2, Apple clang 17.0.0, `clang -O3 bench-pse-apple.c`.

This validates the portability claim in *Non-Bijunctive Permutation Collapse* v2
§6.2 (DOI concept 10.5281/zenodo.18623919): AArch64 `TBL` performs the fused
prune+amplify collapse in a single instruction, on commodity ARM hardware.

## Raw permute throughput

| Operation | Latency | Throughput |
|---|---|---|
| `vqtbl1q` single-source (16B table) | 0.84 ns/op | 1,195 M ops/s |
| **`vqtbl2q` dual-source (32B table, vec_perm equivalent)** | **0.55 ns/op** | **1,834 M ops/s** |

Dual-source is *faster* than single-source on M2 — the wider table does not
cost latency; the instruction pipelines better in this loop.

(The bench's "Float collapse: inf ops/sec" line is a measurement artifact —
the compiler eliminated that loop. Do not quote it.)

## Behavioral divergence (entropy via cntvct_el0)

Same input, five runs. Stock is bit-identical; PSE diverges per run:

| Comparison | Cosine similarity |
|---|---|
| Stock vs stock | 1.000000 |
| PSE run pairs (10 pairs) | 0.867 – 0.897 |

Same behavior class as POWER8 `mftb` entropy: hardware timebase seeds

## Honest caveat: pipeline overhead in isolation

The full collapse pipeline microbench runs at ~0.84x stock (about 16%
overhead) at n=64..16384. The permute is nearly free; the threshold/amplify
passes and pattern generation are the cost. The POWER8 end-to-end speedup
(5.05–8.81x in llama.cpp) came from what collapse enables downstream —
skipped attention paths and cache-resident weights — not from the collapse
loop being faster than a straight pass. The equivalent llama.cpp integration
on ARM (Metal/Accelerate interplay included) is the open next milestone.

## Reproduce

```bash
clang -O3 -o bench-pse bench-pse-apple.c && ./bench-pse
```
